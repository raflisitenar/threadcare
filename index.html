Clothing lasts longer when care starts with the garment, not a universal laundry rule. ThreadCare helps you interpret common care-label categories, understand fabric behavior, respond to everyday marks, and build gentler washing, drying and storage habits.

    02

    Care labels are a sequence of decisions.

    Instead of memorizing every variation at once, recognize the main symbol families first. The exact marks inside or around them add conditions such as temperature, cycle or prohibition.

    ⌒
    Wash

    A wash-tub family symbol communicates whether and how the garment may be washed.

    △
    Bleach

    A triangle family symbol relates to bleaching permissions or restrictions.

    □
    Dry

    A square family symbol covers drying; additional marks indicate the method.

    ○
    Professional care

    A circle family symbol is associated with professional textile-care processes.

    ♨
    Iron

    An iron-shaped symbol communicates ironing permissions and heat conditions.

    The text characters above are category cues, not reproductions of a complete standardized care-label set. Always read the actual garment symbols and accompanying instructions.

    03

    Fabric changes the conversation.

    Fiber is only part of the story. Weave, knit, dye, finish, lining, trim and construction can all affect care.

    COTTON

    Often washable, but not identical.

    Cotton garments can vary widely in colorfastness, shrinkage risk and finish. Follow the garment label rather than assuming every cotton item tolerates hot washing or high drying heat.

    WOOL

    Friction and heat matter.

    Some wool garments require hand washing or professional care, while others have machine-washable treatments. Agitation, temperature changes and unsuitable drying can alter shape or texture.

    LINEN

    Strong fiber, garment-specific care.

    Linen can wrinkle readily and may change dimensions depending on construction and care. Check whether the garment permits machine washing, drying or ironing.

    SYNTHETICS

    Heat sensitivity can vary.

    Polyester, nylon, elastane and other synthetics appear in many blends. Excess heat can be unsuitable for some fibers, elastic components, prints or finishes.

    04

    A better laundry load starts before the machine.

    Sorting and label-checking can prevent more problems than trying to repair damage afterward.

    Read the care label.

    Separate items that require hand washing, professional cleaning, special temperatures or drying restrictions.

    Sort with a purpose.

    Consider color, soil level, fabric weight and lint behavior. A heavy abrasive item may not be a good companion for a delicate knit even if their colors match.

    Check pockets and closures.

    Remove objects, fasten items as appropriate, and follow garment-specific advice for zippers, hooks, drawstrings or turning decorated pieces inside out.

    Use product directions.

    Follow laundry-product dosing and machine instructions. More detergent is not automatically cleaner and can leave unwanted residue.

    Dry according to the garment.

    Drying heat and mechanical action can affect shrinkage, elasticity and shape. Follow the label's drying method rather than applying one setting to every load.

    05

    First response to everyday marks.

    Stains vary by substance and textile. These are conservative starting principles, not guaranteed removal recipes.

    Fresh liquid

    Blot, don't spread. Use a clean absorbent material, work carefully, and check the care label before adding water or treatment.

    Oil or grease

    Avoid aggressive rubbing. Lift excess gently and use a textile-compatible method. Delicate garments may need professional care.

    Unknown mark

    Identify before escalating. Strong heat or an unsuitable product can make some problems harder to correct. Test treatments where appropriate.

    06

    Storage is part of garment care.

    Clean, dry, appropriately supported clothing is generally easier to preserve than clothing stored damp, compressed or under unsuitable conditions.

    Hang selectively

    Structured garments may benefit from suitable hangers, while some heavy knits can stretch under their own weight and are often better folded if their care allows.

    Store dry

    Avoid putting away clothing while damp. Persistent moisture can encourage odor and other problems. Address the moisture source if storage areas remain damp.

    Give items room

    Over-compression can create hard creases and makes inspection difficult. Seasonal storage should still allow you to check items periodically.

    07

    Laundry questions people actually ask.

    Should I wash all clothes before wearing them?

    Needs vary by garment, personal preference and manufacturer guidance. If you choose to wash a new item, follow its care label. Some specialty garments may have specific first-care instructions.

    Does cold water prevent all shrinking?

    No. Fiber, construction, agitation and drying can all affect dimensions. A cooler wash may reduce some risks for some garments, but the care label remains the better guide.

    Can I tumble dry anything labeled machine washable?

    Not necessarily. Washing and drying instructions are separate. A garment may permit machine washing but require line drying, flat drying or another method.

    Why should some sweaters dry flat?

    Wet knitwear can become heavy. Flat drying can help reduce stretching or distortion when the garment label calls for it.

    Is more detergent better for very dirty clothes?

    Not automatically. Use the detergent and machine directions. Excess product can be difficult to rinse and may leave residue; heavily soiled items may instead need an appropriate cycle or pretreatment allowed by the garment label.

    When is professional garment care useful?

    It can be appropriate when the label requires it, or for delicate construction, valuable garments, difficult stains, specialty fibers, linings, embellishments or situations where home treatment could cause damage.
